What's actually possible

Your tools know the pieces.
The Engine connects them.

Most business problems don't live inside one app. They live in the handoff: information copied from one system to another, an approval trapped in email, or a person filling the gap between tools that were never designed to work together.

Below are real examples of those gaps. They aren't a menu of pre-built features—the Engine doesn't have a "vendor coordination module" sitting on a shelf. Describe where the work gets stuck, and it gets built around your business and the tools you already use, not the other way around.

01

QA & compliance data scrubbing

Catching bad, duplicate, or out-of-policy data before it becomes everyone else's problem.

The manual work today

Someone exports data from the ERP, CRM, and spreadsheets, then compares rows against policy rules and reference lists by hand. Errors surface late, and every questionable record starts another email thread.

How the Engine changes it

Run event-driven checks inside your Azure or AWS subscription, verify details through trusted external APIs, and send only the exceptions to Slack or Microsoft Teams. A reviewer approves the fix before the Engine writes it back through the ERP’s internal API, preserving a complete audit trail.

02

Passthrough invoicing & GL entries

Getting costs coded, allocated, and posted correctly without someone re-keying every line.

The manual work today

A vendor invoice arrives by email, someone re-keys every line, looks up the customer or project, calculates the passthrough, and chooses a GL account. Another person checks the spreadsheet before anything can be posted.

How the Engine changes it

The Engine reads the invoice, matches it to project and customer data through your ERP API, and proposes the allocation and GL coding. Approval happens in Teams or Slack; then n8n or an event-bus workflow posts the approved entries and attaches the source document.

03

External approvals, orchestrated by email

Chasing down sign-off from people who don't log into your system, without it living in someone's inbox.

The manual work today

Documents go to customers, vendors, or board members by email, and a coordinator chases replies while sorting approvals from questions and outdated versions. The real status lives in one person’s inbox.

How the Engine changes it

Give each recipient a secure one-click approval link—or let them reply by email or SMS through Twilio—without creating another account. The Engine records the decision, sends reminders, routes questions to a person, and updates the source system through its API.

04

Training & learning management

Keeping track of who's trained, who's overdue, and what they still need to complete.

The manual work today

Training records sit across spreadsheets, an LMS, and managers’ inboxes, while supervisors manually identify who is overdue. The same generic course is assigned even when roles, locations, or policy changes differ.

How the Engine changes it

Build role-specific micro-lessons from approved material and deliver them through Teams, Slack, email, or short ElevenLabs audio. Completion and quiz results flow back to the LMS or HR system, while managers see only overdue or uncertain cases that need attention.

05

Vendor coordination

Keeping multiple vendors, timelines, and handoffs from quietly falling out of sync.

The manual work today

A project lead checks vendor portals, email chains, calls, and spreadsheets to reconstruct what is on schedule. A missed dependency often becomes visible only when the next vendor cannot start.

How the Engine changes it

Use n8n to watch shared mailboxes and vendor APIs, normalize each update onto an event bus in your Azure or AWS environment, and maintain one live handoff view. The Engine posts concise Teams or Slack updates, texts overdue vendors through Twilio, and flags dependencies that need a human decision.

06

Field compliance, from a phone

Giving field staff a simple way to follow process correctly, without a full-time trainer riding along.

The manual work today

Field staff carry paper forms or take loose photos, then reconstruct the visit later when details are already fading. Missing signatures, locations, or equipment readings send the back office into another round of calls and re-entry.

How the Engine changes it

A focused phone app uses the camera, barcode scanning, geolocation, timestamps, and offline storage to guide the work and capture proof as it happens. It checks the job against an internal ERP or work-order API, catches missing steps on site, and syncs the completed record when connectivity returns.

07

Breaking free of niche vendor lock-in

When you're stuck doing things one provider's way because that's the only way anyone's ever shown you—until it isn't.

The manual work today

Critical data is trapped behind a niche portal, rigid export, or aging device, so teams copy it elsewhere and shape their process around the vendor’s limitations. Every useful change depends on that vendor’s price, roadmap, and willingness to help.

How the Engine changes it

Bring permitted API calls, scheduled exports, email intake, and local device data into a customer-owned layer in Azure or AWS, then rebuild the few workflows that actually matter as small web or mobile tools. For older on-site equipment, even a Raspberry Pi can bridge serial ports or sensors to the event bus—without replacing the machine that still works.

These are starting points, not fixed packages. If your situation looks like a mashup of two of them—or nothing like any of them—that's normal. Bring it to me anyway.
